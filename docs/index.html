HOW TO READ THIS DASHBOARD

How We Measure Bilateral Relationships

Primary Legal Sources: Every benefit is backed by published statutory acts, treaties, or ministerial notifications.

Every bilateral relationship is scored from Level 1 to 3 based on how deeply the two nations grant mutual or unilateral benefits to each other's citizens, students, and businesses:

Level 1 Regional Baseline (1 pt)

Standard South Asian agreements available broadly across all member states (e.g., standard visa exemption schemes, basic transit windows).

Level 2 Substantial Privilege (2 pts)

Special cost savings and reserved access (e.g., domestic university tuition fees, tourist fee waivers, central bank currency credit lines).

Level 3 Deep Bilateral Treaty (3 pts)

Highest level of cross-border partnership (e.g., free movement of citizens, equal work and residency rights without requiring a visa).

METHODOLOGY & TAXONOMY

South Asian Bilateral Relations & Benefits Index

This open research index tracks and quantifies preferential treatments, bilateral treaties, and reciprocal privileges exchanged among South Asian nations (Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka).

Benefit Level Weightings

Level 1 Regional Baseline (1 pt)

Standard agreements, baseline visa schemes (SVES), or multilateral exemptions applicable broadly across members.

Level 2 Substantial Concession (2 pts)

Subsidized university tuition (e.g. SAU domestic rates), tourist levy exemptions (Bhutan SDF), bilateral currency swap windows, or port transshipment priorities.

Level 3 Deep Bilateral Treaty (3 pts)

Unrestricted freedom of movement, national work/residency treatment, or comprehensive free transit corridors (e.g. India-Nepal 1950, India-Bhutan 1949/2007).

Official Legal Sources

Every concession links directly to verifiable statutory acts, Ministry of External Affairs publications, central bank regulations, or institutional charters.
